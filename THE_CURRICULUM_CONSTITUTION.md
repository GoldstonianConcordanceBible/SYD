# SYD CURRICULUM CONSTITUTION
## One Standard for Every Course
SYD courses are modular learning objects.
Each course should be independently understandable, assessable, versioned, and auditable.
The purpose of this curriculum constitution is to prevent every new idea from requiring a new educational architecture.
The subject may change.
The operating structure remains consistent.
---

# MICROCOURSE PRINCIPLE
The smallest useful educational object wins.
Do not create a four-hour course when one clear concept can be taught and assessed in twenty minutes.
Examples:
"What Is a Wallet?"
can stand alone.
"What Is a Long Thesis?"
can stand alone.
"What Is a Hash?"
can stand alone.
"How Does an Oracle Feed Stock Prices On-Chain?"
can stand alone.
Microcourses may later stack into larger pathways.
---
# THE STUDENT CONTROLS DEPTH
The learner may take:
ONE COURSE
or
A STACK.
Do not require a learner to buy unrelated material merely to access the one thing they want to learn.
---
# ACADEMIC PRINCIPLE
A course is not complete merely because videos exist.
A formal SYD course requires evidence that the learner can demonstrate the stated outcome.
Therefore:
VIDEO ≠ COURSE
CONTENT + OUTCOME + ACTIVITY + ASSESSMENT + EVIDENCE = COURSE
---
# TOKEN FIREWALL
Buying, holding, or staking any token does not satisfy a learning outcome.
Academic progress follows assessment.
---
# REUSE
The same curriculum constitution should be usable across:
SydTek Scholars
SydTek University pathways
SydTek Seminary
GCB
Golgotha
THE HOUSE
future licensed educational partners.
---
# DOCTRINE
## BUILD ONCE.
## REUSE THE STANDARD.
## VERSION THE CHANGE.
## PRESERVE THE EVIDENCE.

# COURSE ID STANDARD
## Purpose
Every formal course receives a stable identifier.
The ID should tell us:
- institution/ecosystem,
- subject family,
- sequence.
---
# FORMAT
Use:
`SYD-[DOMAIN]-[NUMBER]`
Examples:
`SYD-MKT-101`
`SYD-TRD-401`
`SYD-CHAIN-501`
`SYD-BTC-600`
`SYD-ETH-700`
`SYD-SOL-800`
`SYD-TKN-901`
---
# INITIAL DOMAIN CODES

Markets.
Example:
`SYD-MKT-101`
S&P 500 Long.

## CHAIN

---

## ETH

---
## SOL

---
## TKN

---

---

---

---
## GCB
Goldstonian Concordance Bible educational material.
---
## SEM
SydTek Seminary.
---
# NUMBERING
The numbers do not automatically represent traditional college course levels.
They primarily establish sequence and domain organization during the Scholars phase.
Suggested broad ranges:
000–099
Onboarding / orientation.
100–299
Foundation.
300–499
Applied.
500–699
Infrastructure / intermediate.
700–899
Advanced domain laboratories.
900–999
Ecosystem experiments / capstones / specialized labs.
This may evolve.
---
# STABILITY
Once a course ID is publicly released, avoid changing the ID merely because the title changes slightly.
Example:
`SYD-MKT-101`
may move from:
"Trading the S&P 500: The Long Thesis"
to:
"S&P 500 Long Thesis Lab"
without necessarily changing the ID.
Version the course instead.
---
# NEW COURSE VS NEW VERSION
Create a new ID when:
- the educational objective is materially different,
- the audience is materially different,
- the assessment is fundamentally different.
Create a new version when:
- lessons are improved,
- examples change,
- assessments are refined,
- technology changes,
- cases are updated.
---
# RULE
## THE ID IDENTIFIES THE COURSE.
## THE VERSION IDENTIFIES THE STATE OF THE COURSE.

FILE 3 — curriculum/COURSE_VERSION_STANDARD.md

# COURSE VERSION STANDARD
## Format
Use semantic-style versioning:
`MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH`
Example:
`1.2.3`
Full reference:
`SYD-MKT-101 v1.2.3`
---
# GENESIS VERSION
A new course begins as:
`v0.1.0`
This means:
experimental / early development.
---
# MAJOR VERSION
Example:
`v2.0.0`
Use when:
- learning outcomes materially change,
- assessment architecture fundamentally changes,
- the course becomes a substantially different educational object,
- formal academic mapping requires a major redesign.
---
# MINOR VERSION
Example:
`v1.1.0`
Use when:
- a new module is added,
- substantial content is added,
- a major case study is added,
- learner feedback causes meaningful revision.
---
# PATCH VERSION
Example:
`v1.0.1`
Use for:
- typo corrections,
- broken links,
- formatting,
- minor clarification,
- factual corrections that do not alter the educational structure.
---
# VERSION RECORD
Every course should contain:
`CHANGELOG.md`
Example:
## v0.2.0
Added:
- invalidation exercise,
- new S&P case screenshot,
- revised rubric.
Reason:
Pilot learners could identify direction but struggled to define what would make the thesis wrong.
---
# VERSION + LEARNER RECORD
A learner completion record should identify the version completed.
Example:
Learner completed:
`SYD-MKT-101 v1.1.0`
This matters because later versions may differ.
---
# GITHUB
Git commits preserve granular change history.
Course versions mark meaningful educational releases.
The two systems complement each other.
---
# RULE
## DO NOT SILENTLY REPLACE THE COURSE.
## VERSION IT.

FILE 4 — curriculum/MICROCOURSE_STANDARD.md

# SYD MICROCOURSE STANDARD
## Purpose
The microcourse is the primary learning unit inside SydTek Scholars.
It should be:
- narrow,
- useful,
- independently purchasable where appropriate,
- assessable,
- stackable.
---
# TARGET SIZE
Recommended initial range:
## 15–45 MINUTES OF INSTRUCTION
Typical structure:
3–6 lessons.
Typical individual lesson:
3–8 minutes.
These are design targets, not rigid academic laws.
The learning outcome determines the appropriate length.
---
# MICROCOURSE FORMULA
HOOK / PROBLEM
↓
CONCEPT
↓
EXAMPLE
↓
APPLICATION
↓
ASSESSMENT
↓
EVIDENCE
---
# REQUIRED COMPONENTS
A standard microcourse should contain:
## 1. COURSE OVERVIEW
What will the learner be able to do?
## 2. LEARNING OUTCOMES
Usually 2–5 measurable outcomes.
## 3. MODULES
Short lessons.
## 4. KNOWLEDGE CHECK
Short formative check.
## 5. APPLIED ACTIVITY
The learner does something.
## 6. FINAL ASSESSMENT
Evidence of mastery.
## 7. RUBRIC
How work will be judged.
## 8. COMPLETION RECORD
Evidence that the learner completed the requirement.
---
# OPTIONAL COMPONENTS
- Telegram discussion
- YouTube video
- Substack reading
- GitHub exercise
- code lab
- wallet lab
- market simulation
- research artifact
- peer review
- live session
- portfolio artifact.
---
# MARKET COURSE EXAMPLE
## Course
`SYD-TRD-408`
Invalidation.
### Lesson 1
What is invalidation?
### Lesson 2
Why a thesis needs a failure condition.
### Lesson 3
S&P example.
### Lesson 4
Moderna example.
### Applied task
Given three market theses, define what evidence would invalidate each.
### Final evidence
Learner submits one fully structured thesis with explicit invalidation.
---
# COURSE STACKING
Multiple microcourses may form a stack.
Example:
MARKET FOUNDATIONS
could include:
- Candlesticks
- Trend
- Support / Resistance
- Volume
- Invalidation
- Position Sizing
- Journal
---
# DO NOT PAD CONTENT
A course should not be longer simply to justify a higher price.
Educational value is not measured by runtime alone.
---
# STANDARD
## TEACH ONE USEFUL THING WELL.
## PROVE THE LEARNER CAN USE IT.
## LET THEM MOVE ON.

FILE 5 — curriculum/LEARNING_OUTCOMES_STANDARD.md

# LEARNING OUTCOMES STANDARD
## Purpose
Every formal course should state what the learner should be able to do after completing it.
Avoid vague outcomes.
---
# WEAK OUTCOMES
Avoid:
"Understand trading."
"Know blockchain."
"Learn Bitcoin."
"Be familiar with risk."
These are difficult to assess.
---
# STRONG OUTCOMES
Prefer measurable verbs.
Examples:
IDENTIFY
DEFINE
EXPLAIN
COMPARE
APPLY
CONSTRUCT
ANALYZE
EVALUATE
DEMONSTRATE
DOCUMENT
DESIGN
BUILD
---
# EXAMPLE — S&P 500 COURSE
By completion, the learner will be able to:
1. explain what the S&P 500 represents,
2. distinguish an index from a tradeable instrument,
3. construct a bounded long thesis,
4. define thesis invalidation,
5. perform an after-action review.
---
# COURSE LEARNING OUTCOMES
Abbreviation:
## CLO
Example:
`CLO-1`
Explain what the S&P 500 represents.
---
# PROGRAM LEARNING OUTCOMES
Abbreviation:
## PLO
Example:
`PLO-2`
Construct and document a market thesis including risk and invalidation.
---
# MAPPING
Courses should later map CLOs to PLOs.
Example:
| Course | CLO | Program Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| SYD-MKT-101 | CLO-1 | PLO-1 |
| SYD-MKT-101 | CLO-3 | PLO-2 |
| SYD-MKT-101 | CLO-5 | PLO-4 |
---
# ASSESSABILITY
For every outcome ask:
> What evidence would prove this learner can do this?
If there is no answer, revise the outcome.
---
# COURSE SIZE
Most microcourses should have:
2–5 CLOs.
Do not create fifteen outcomes for a twenty-minute course.
---
# BLOOM-LIKE PROGRESSION
Foundation:
identify  
define  
explain
Applied:
apply  
construct  
document
Advanced:
analyze  
evaluate  
design  
build
---
# STANDARD
## IF YOU CANNOT ASSESS THE OUTCOME, IT IS NOT YET A USEFUL OUTCOME.

FILE 6 — curriculum/ASSESSMENT_STANDARD.md

# SYD ASSESSMENT STANDARD
## Purpose
Assessment determines whether the learner demonstrated the learning outcomes.
Watching content is not sufficient evidence by itself.
---
# ASSESSMENT TYPES
## KNOWLEDGE CHECK
Low-stakes formative assessment.
Examples:
- multiple choice,
- true/false,
- matching,
- short response.
---
## APPLIED ANALYSIS
Learner analyzes a case.
Example:
Review the Moderna case and identify:
- thesis,
- catalyst,
- invalidation,
- risk.
---
## DEMONSTRATION
Learner performs a task.
Examples:
- connect a test wallet,
- explain a transaction,
- demonstrate a dApp,
- execute a simulation.
---
## BUILD
Learner creates something.
Examples:
- smart contract,
- DAO constitution,
- token-gated community,
- decentralized application.
---
## PORTFOLIO ARTIFACT
Learner produces something that can remain part of a professional portfolio.
---
## ORAL DEFENSE
Learner explains their own work.
Useful where AI-assisted work is permitted.
---
# ASSESSMENT ALIGNMENT
Every major assessment should map to specific CLOs.
Example:
Final Market Thesis:
CLO-2  
CLO-3  
CLO-4
---
# REAL MONEY IS NOT REQUIRED FOR MASTERY
A learner should not need to risk meaningful capital merely to pass a course.
Where possible use:
- simulation,
- paper trading,
- testnet,
- sandbox,
- historical data,
- low-risk demonstration.
---
# MARKET OUTCOME ≠ ASSESSMENT RESULT
A profitable trade does not automatically mean good analysis.
A losing trade does not automatically mean poor analysis.
Assessment focuses on:
- thesis quality,
- risk definition,
- process,
- evidence,
- reflection.
---
# RESUBMISSION
SydTek Scholars may use mastery-based resubmission.
Suggested process:
SUBMIT
↓
REVIEW
↓
FEEDBACK
↓
REVISE
↓
RESUBMIT
---
# AI
Where AI use is permitted:
The learner remains responsible for:
- factual accuracy,
- evidence,
- explanation,
- final submission.
An instructor may require oral defense.
---
# DEFAULT PASSING STANDARD
Unless otherwise stated:
## 80%
or equivalent mastery classification.
---
# STANDARD
## ASSESS THE SKILL.
## NOT THE ABILITY TO SIT THROUGH THE VIDEO.

FILE 7 — curriculum/RUBRIC_STANDARD.md

# SYD RUBRIC STANDARD
## Purpose
A rubric explains what successful evidence looks like.
Rubrics should be visible before submission where practical.
---
# DEFAULT FOUR-LEVEL SCALE
## 4 — EXEMPLARY
Clear mastery.
Accurate.
Well evidenced.
Independent application.
---
## 3 — PROFICIENT
Meets expected course standard.
Minor weaknesses do not materially affect competence.
---
## 2 — DEVELOPING
Partial competence.
Important gaps remain.
---
## 1 — INSUFFICIENT
Learning outcome not adequately demonstrated.
---
# DEFAULT DIMENSIONS
Depending upon the course:
CONCEPT ACCURACY
EVIDENCE
APPLICATION
RISK / LIMITATIONS
DOCUMENTATION
REFLECTION
ACADEMIC INTEGRITY
TECHNICAL FUNCTIONALITY
---
# MARKET CASE RUBRIC EXAMPLE
| Criterion | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Instrument identification | Exact instrument identified | Mostly clear | Ambiguous | Incorrect |
| Thesis | Specific and evidence-based | Clear | Partial | Missing |
| Invalidation | Explicit and testable | Present | Weak | Missing |
| Risk | Material risks identified | Adequate | Limited | Missing |
| Review | Separates process from outcome | Adequate | Generic | Missing |
---
# BUILD COURSE RUBRIC
For technical builds, evaluate:
- functionality,
- architecture,
- security,
- documentation,
- testing,
- explanation.
---
# PASSING
The rubric should map to the passing rule.
Example:
Minimum:
80%
and no score of `1` on a critical safety criterion.
---
# CRITICAL CRITERIA
Certain outcomes may be mandatory regardless of total score.
Examples:
- never exposing a private key,
- identifying the actual financial instrument,
- respecting an NIL restriction,
- avoiding fabricated evidence.
---
# STANDARD
## THE LEARNER SHOULD KNOW HOW SUCCESS WILL BE JUDGED BEFORE THEY SUBMIT.

## Purpose
SYD should document actual estimated learner effort.

- course design,
- workload planning,
- certificate stacks,
- future academic review.
---
# LEARNING HOURS MAY INCLUDE
Video instruction.
Reading.
Practice.
Discussion.
Simulation.
Lab work.
Assessment.
Revision.
Project work.
---
# DO NOT COUNT ONLY VIDEO LENGTH
A 20-minute video plus:
20 minutes reading,
30 minutes analysis,
30 minutes assessment
may represent:
100 minutes of learner effort.
---
# MICROCOURSE EXAMPLE
Instruction:
25 minutes.
Reading:
10 minutes.
Applied exercise:
20 minutes.
Assessment:
20 minutes.
Expected learner effort:
75 minutes.
---
# ESTIMATE HONESTLY
Do not inflate hours simply to make a course appear academically larger.
Collect learner data later and revise estimates.
---
# FUTURE EVIDENCE
Track:
estimated hours
versus
actual learner-reported completion time.
This can become part of curriculum research.

# STANDARD
## MEASURE LEARNER EFFORT.
## DO NOT EQUATE RUNTIME WITH LEARNING.

# SYD PRICING STANDARD
## Purpose
Pricing should preserve the modular philosophy.
Learners should be able to buy the thing they want without being forced into a giant bundle.
---
# INITIAL PLANNING BANDS
These are starting hypotheses, not permanent prices.
## FREE
Orientation / onboarding / promotional foundation.
Example:
`SYD-MKT-000`
---
## $7–$12
Small concept microcourse.
---
## $19–$29
Standard foundation microcourse.
---
## $39–$49
Applied course.
---
## $49–$99
Lab.
---
## $99+
Build course / capstone / intensive project.
---
# PRICE IS NOT BASED ONLY ON VIDEO LENGTH
Pricing may consider:
- transformation,
- instructor expertise,
- assessment,
- feedback,
- lab infrastructure,
- reusable resources,
- support,
- credential value.
---
# BUNDLES
Courses may be bundled.
Example:
Individual total:
$60
Bundle:
$49
But individual purchase should remain available when practical.
---
# TOKEN ACCESS
A token may unlock:
- Telegram,
- community,
- specific experiences,
- discounts.
Do not automatically require token ownership for ordinary academic completion unless the course genuinely requires that technical experience.
---
# SCHOLARSHIPS / ACCESS
Future policies may support:
- scholarships,
- sponsored seats,
- institutional licenses,
- cohort pricing.
---
# TEST PRICE WITH EVIDENCE
Track:
- page visits,
- purchases,
- refunds,
- completion,
- learner satisfaction.
A price is a hypothesis until learners validate it.
---
# RULE
## SELL THE NEXT THING THE LEARNER WANTS TO LEARN.
